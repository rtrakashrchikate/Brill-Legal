Few legal processes are as personal as a divorce. The law itself is manageable — the harder part is making sound decisions under emotional strain. This guide explains both routes to divorce, the money and custody questions that travel with them, and realistic timelines, so you can make decisions with a clear map. It anchors our [private client and family practice](/practices/private-client).

## The two routes: mutual consent and contested

**Mutual consent divorce** (Section 13B, Hindu Marriage Act; Section 28, Special Marriage Act) is available when both spouses agree the marriage should end and settle the consequences — maintenance or a one-time settlement, child arrangements, and division of belongings. The procedure is two court motions separated by a cooling-off period of six months, which the Supreme Court has held courts may waive where reconciliation is clearly impossible. It is faster, cheaper and far less corrosive than a contested case.

**Contested divorce** is filed by one spouse on statutory grounds and proceeds like a trial: pleadings, evidence, cross-examination, judgment. It commonly takes two to five years and can be longer with appeals. A contested filing is sometimes unavoidable — but it is also a negotiating reality that most contested cases eventually settle into consent terms.

## Grounds for contested divorce

Under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955 (similar grounds appear in other personal laws and the Special Marriage Act):

- **Cruelty** — physical or mental. Mental cruelty is assessed cumulatively: sustained humiliation, false criminal complaints, public abuse, denial of companionship. It is the most litigated ground.
- **Desertion** — abandonment without reasonable cause for two years or more.
- **Adultery** — a single act suffices as a ground, though proving it is rarely straightforward.
- **Conversion, mental disorder, communicable disease, renunciation, presumption of death** — narrower grounds, less frequently invoked.

The petition's drafting matters enormously: vague allegations invite dismissal, while a precise, dated narrative of incidents — supported by messages, medical records or complaints — gives the case its spine.

## Maintenance and alimony

Money questions arise at three stages:

1. **Interim maintenance** — monthly support while the case is pending, so neither spouse is starved into settlement.
2. **Permanent alimony** — a monthly amount or one-time lump sum at decree.
3. **Child support** — independent of the spouses' claims; the child's education and upbringing costs are shared by both parents per capacity.

Courts weigh income and assets of both sides, the marriage's standard of living, the claimant's earning capacity, and conduct. Following Supreme Court directions, both parties must file detailed **affidavits of assets and income** — accuracy in these affidavits is taken seriously, and concealment damages credibility across the whole case.

## Children: custody and access

Custody is governed by one principle — the **welfare of the child** — and not by either parent's "right." In practice:

- Young children usually remain in the primary care of the mother, but this is a working presumption, not a rule.
- The non-custodial parent ordinarily receives structured access: weekends, holidays, video calls.
- Courts increasingly approve **joint parenting plans** where parents can cooperate.
- An older child's own preference carries weight.

Custody orders are never final in the way money decrees are — they can be revisited as circumstances change. The most durable arrangements are the ones parents design themselves and the court endorses.

## Domestic violence and connected proceedings

Family disputes often produce parallel proceedings: a complaint under the **Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005** (residence orders, protection orders, monetary relief), maintenance petitions under Section 144 BNSS, and sometimes criminal complaints. Each forum has its own pace and purpose, and statements made in one will be read in the others — coordinated strategy across them is essential, the same discipline we apply in [civil litigation](/insights/civil-litigation-india-guide).

## What a well-run divorce looks like

From our practice, the matters that conclude fastest and cleanest share three features:

1. **An early, honest assessment** of the realistic settlement range — courts rarely produce outcomes far from it, only slower.
2. **Complete financial disclosure**, prepared once and prepared properly.
3. **Children insulated** from the litigation — courts notice, and it serves everyone.
