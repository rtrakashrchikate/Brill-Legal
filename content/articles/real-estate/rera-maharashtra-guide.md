The Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016 changed the balance of power between homebuyers and builders. In Maharashtra — RERA's busiest jurisdiction by far — MahaRERA has registered tens of thousands of projects and decided a large body of complaints, building practical jurisprudence on delay, refunds and defective construction. This guide explains how the system works and how to use it well. It anchors our [real estate and construction practice](/practices/real-estate).

## What RERA covers — and what it doesn't

RERA applies to residential and commercial projects above threshold size (over 500 sq m or more than eight units), which must register with MahaRERA before being marketed. Registration obliges the promoter to:

- Disclose approved plans, timelines and title details on the public portal
- Keep 70% of buyer receipts in a designated project account
- Deliver possession by the committed date or face interest and refund liability
- Fix structural defects reported within five years of possession

RERA does **not** cover completed projects that received their occupancy certificate before registration was required, most resale transactions between individuals, or pure title disputes — those remain with the civil courts or consumer fora. Choosing the right forum is itself a strategic decision; sometimes a [consumer complaint](/practices/tribunal) or civil suit serves better.

## Before you buy: the five-minute MahaRERA check

Every registered project has a public page on the MahaRERA portal. Before paying a rupee, check:

1. **Registration status** — valid, lapsed, or revoked
2. **The committed possession date** — the legally operative date, whatever the brochure says
3. **Quarterly progress updates** — stale updates are a warning sign
4. **Litigation disclosures** — pending complaints against the project
5. **Sanctioned plans** — match what you are being shown

Pair this with independent [title due diligence](/insights/buying-property-india-checklist) — RERA registration is not a title guarantee.

## Delayed possession: your two options under Section 18

Delay is the most common dispute. If the builder misses the agreement date, RERA gives you a clean choice:

**Exit.** Withdraw from the project and claim a **full refund with interest** (MahaRERA pegs interest at SBI MCLR + 2%) plus compensation. The right is not defeated by the builder's excuses about approvals or force majeure unless genuinely made out.

**Stay.** Keep the booking and claim **interest for every month of delay** until possession is delivered. Many buyers in nearly complete projects choose this — the project finishes, and the delay is compensated.

MahaRERA and the Bombay High Court have repeatedly held that the agreement date binds the promoter, and that one-sided clauses purporting to extend it indefinitely do not save the builder.

## Filing a complaint: what to expect

The complaint is filed online (Form A) with a modest fee. Orders typically issue within months — far faster than civil litigation. Three practical notes from our practice:

- **Plead the relief precisely.** Refund-with-interest and interest-for-delay are different remedies under Section 18; choose deliberately, because switching later wastes time.
- **Document the demand trail.** Letters and emails calling on the builder to deliver strengthen both the claim and the interest calculation.
- **Anticipate the moratorium defence.** Builders routinely invoke COVID-period extensions granted by MahaRERA; the arithmetic of those extensions is often where the case is won.

Orders are enforceable as land revenue, and persistent non-compliance can lead to penalties and even de-registration of the project.

## Appeals and beyond

Either party may appeal to the **Maharashtra Real Estate Appellate Tribunal** within 60 days; a builder appealing a refund order must first deposit the amount ordered. A further appeal on questions of law lies to the **Bombay High Court**. Where a builder has entered insolvency, homebuyers act as financial creditors before the [NCLT](/practices/tribunal) — a parallel track with its own strategy.
