Registering a private limited company is the starting point for most entrepreneurs, technology founders, and businesses seeking to raise external capital in India. The Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) administers incorporation through the MCA21 portal, and the SPICe+ form has consolidated what was once a multi-step process into a single integrated application. Understanding what is involved, from obtaining digital credentials through to the first post-incorporation compliance obligations, helps founders approach the process efficiently and avoid common errors that delay the certificate of incorporation.

## Why Choose a Private Limited Company

The private limited structure offers limited liability: shareholders are not personally liable for the debts and obligations of the company beyond the amount unpaid on their shares. The company has separate legal personality and perpetual succession, meaning its existence is not affected by changes in ownership or management. The structure is investor-ready: external investors, venture capital funds, and angel investors are familiar and comfortable with equity investment in a private limited company.

Compared with an LLP, the private limited company has higher annual compliance obligations but offers greater flexibility for equity-based compensation (employee stock options), external investment, and eventual public listing. Compared with a proprietorship or partnership firm, the private limited company's limited liability and separate personality make it a significantly stronger vehicle for any business with meaningful contractual obligations or financial exposure.

## Step One: Digital Signature Certificate and Director Identification Number

Every proposed director must have a Director Identification Number (DIN), issued by the MCA. For new incorporations, DINs for up to three directors can be applied for directly through the SPICe+ form itself. Directors who already have a DIN from a previous directorship use their existing number.

A Digital Signature Certificate (DSC) is required for each director, subscriber, and professional certifying the application. The DSC is a cryptographic credential used to sign electronic filings with the MCA. It is issued by MCA-certified agencies such as eMudhra, Sify, and nCode against identity and address proof. Obtaining a DSC typically takes one to two working days. Every person signing the SPICe+ form must have a valid Class III DSC at the time of submission.

## Step Two: Name Reservation

A company's name must satisfy the requirements of the Companies (Incorporation) Rules, 2014. It must not be identical or deceptively similar to an existing company or LLP, must not be prohibited or offensive, must not imply government connection without approval, and should be relevant to the company's proposed objects.

Name reservation can be done through the RUN (Reserve Unique Name) service, which allows two name choices, or directly through SPICe+ Part A. The MCA's company name search tool should be used before submitting to check for conflicts. Common reasons for name rejection include excessive genericity (for example, a name consisting only of common words), names that are too close to well-known trademarks, and names suggesting activities not permitted to a private limited company. Rejection means reapplying and restarting the clock, so spending time on name selection before submission is worthwhile.

## Step Three: Drafting the MoA and AoA

The Memorandum of Association is the company's charter document. It sets out the company's name, the state of its registered office, the objects for which the company is incorporated, and the liability clause. For most companies, the objects clause is drafted broadly to capture all anticipated business activities and ancillary matters, to avoid the need for future amendment as the business evolves.

The Articles of Association govern the internal management of the company: the rights and obligations of shareholders, procedures for share transfers and transmission, pre-emption rights, composition and procedures of the board, quorum requirements, reserved matters requiring special majority or shareholder approval, and other governance provisions. The Companies Act, 2013 provides Table F as a model AoA, but most companies, particularly those with multiple founders or anticipated external investors, adopt a bespoke AoA from incorporation that reflects their specific governance arrangements.

## Step Four: The SPICe+ Filing

SPICe+ is an integrated web form comprising several components. SPICe+ Part A handles name reservation if not done separately via RUN. SPICe+ Part B captures the company's incorporation details, the directors' and subscribers' particulars, and the registered office address. The AGILE-PRO-S form, filed simultaneously, handles applications for GSTIN, EPFO and ESIC registration, Professional Tax registration in states where applicable, and the opening of a bank account with a linked bank.

The e-MoA and e-AoA are filed as electronic constitutional documents linked to the SPICe+ submission. All directors and subscribers sign using their DSCs. The application, together with the prescribed Registrar of Companies fee based on the authorised capital, is submitted to the MCA portal for processing.

## Step Five: Certificate of Incorporation

If the Registrar of Companies is satisfied with the filing, the Certificate of Incorporation (CoI) is issued electronically. The CoI confirms the company's name, its Corporate Identity Number (CIN), and the date of incorporation. PAN and TAN are issued simultaneously through the AGILE-PRO-S form. From the date shown on the CoI, the company exists as a separate legal entity.

## Post-Incorporation Steps

Opening a current bank account is the immediate priority. The bank requires the CoI, the PAN, the AoA and MoA, and a certified copy of the board resolution authorising the account signatories. The first board meeting must be held within 30 days of incorporation and must address the appointment of the first auditor, noting the registered office, and any other preliminary matters.

GST registration becomes mandatory when annual turnover crosses the applicable threshold, currently twenty lakh rupees for services in most states and forty lakh rupees for goods, or immediately for e-commerce sellers, businesses making inter-state taxable supplies, and those voluntarily registering. MSME registration under the Udyam portal is optional but beneficial: it provides access to government procurement preferences, credit guarantee schemes, and the delayed payment protections under the MSMED Act.

The Shops and Establishments Act registration with the local municipal authority is required before commencing business operations.

## Annual Compliance Obligations

A private limited company has mandatory annual filings with the MCA regardless of the level of its activity. Form AOC-4 (annual financial statements and auditor's report) and Form MGT-7 (annual return) must be filed each year within the prescribed timelines after the close of the financial year. Directors are required to update their KYC through DIR-3 KYC annually.

The board of directors must meet at least four times in each financial year, with a gap of no more than 120 days between any two consecutive meetings. A statutory audit by a Chartered Accountant is mandatory for every financial year. The company must also comply with income tax filing obligations and, if GST registered, with GST return filing requirements.
