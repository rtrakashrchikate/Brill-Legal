Registering a private limited company in India is a structured process administered by the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) through its online portal. The SPICe+ (Simplified Proforma for Incorporating Company Electronically Plus) form consolidates what used to be multiple separate applications into a single integrated filing.

## Why a private limited company

Before the mechanics of registration, the entity choice: a private limited company is the standard structure for businesses that intend to raise external capital, bring in co-founders, or operate at any scale. It offers limited liability (shareholders are not personally liable for the company's debts beyond their investment), separate legal personality, perpetual succession, and a well-understood governance framework. An LLP may be preferable for professional services firms or where the partners want greater flexibility; a One Person Company (OPC) suits a sole founder at early stage. Our [guide to starting a business in India](/insights/starting-business-india-guide) compares the options.

## Step 1: Director Identification Numbers and Digital Signatures

Every proposed director must have a **Director Identification Number (DIN)**, issued by the MCA. For new incorporations, DINs can be applied for through SPICe+ itself (for up to three directors). For existing directors (who already have a DIN), the existing number is used.

Each director must also have a **Digital Signature Certificate (DSC)** — a cryptographic credential used to sign electronic filings. DSCs are issued by MCA-certified agencies (eMudhra, Sify, nCode, and others) against identity and address proof. The process takes 1-2 days.

## Step 2: Name reservation

A company's name must be unique, not deceptively similar to existing companies, not prohibited by the Names Rules, and relevant to the company's objects. Name reservation can be done through the **RUN (Reserve Unique Name)** service (two name choices) or through the SPICe+ form directly.

The MCA's search tool allows checking existing names. Common errors — generic words, names too close to trademarks or well-known companies, names implying government connection — result in rejection and restart the clock.

## Step 3: SPICe+ filing

The SPICe+ form integrates:

- **SPICe+ Part A**: Name reservation (if not done separately via RUN)
- **SPICe+ Part B**: Company incorporation details, director particulars, registered office address, subscriber details
- **AGILE-PRO-S**: Simultaneous applications for GSTIN, EPFO/ESIC registration, Professional Tax registration (state-specific), and opening of a bank account
- **e-MoA and e-AoA**: The constitutional documents in electronic form

The form is uploaded to the MCA portal, signed by all directors and subscribers using their DSCs, and submitted with the applicable Registrar of Companies fee.

## Step 4: MoA and AoA

The Memorandum of Association states the company's name, registered state, objects (principal and incidental), and the liability clause. For most companies, the objects clause is drafted broadly to avoid the need for future amendment.

The Articles of Association govern the company internally. The Companies Act 2013 provides a Table F as a model AoA, but most companies adopt a bespoke AoA from incorporation, particularly where there are multiple founders or where investor rights will need to be accommodated. Key provisions: share transfer restrictions, pre-emption rights, quorum, board composition, and reserved matters requiring shareholder approval.

## Step 5: Certificate of Incorporation and PAN/TAN

If the Registrar of Companies approves the filing, the Certificate of Incorporation (CoI) is issued electronically. The CoI is the company's birth certificate — it confirms the company's name, CIN (Corporate Identification Number), and date of incorporation. PAN and TAN are issued simultaneously through the AGILE-PRO-S form.

## Post-incorporation steps

**Bank account**: The company must open a current account in its name. The CoI, PAN, and board resolution authorising signatories are required.

**First board meeting**: Within 30 days of incorporation, the first board meeting must be held to appoint an auditor, note the registered office address, and address any other initial matters.

**GST registration**: Required once turnover crosses the threshold (currently Rs. 20 lakh for most services, Rs. 40 lakh for goods) or immediately for e-commerce sellers and inter-state suppliers.

**MSME registration**: Optional but beneficial for access to government schemes, delayed payment protections under MSMED Act, and procurement preferences.

**Annual compliance**: Annual filings with the MCA (AOC-4 for accounts, MGT-7 for annual return), board meetings (minimum four per year), statutory audit, and income tax return are mandatory from the first year.
