Starting a business involves a series of legal decisions that are cheap to make correctly at the beginning and expensive to fix later: the entity, the equity, the agreements, the registrations. This guide maps the full sequence for an Indian start — from choosing a structure to the compliance rhythm of a running company. It anchors our [corporate and commercial practice](/practices/corporate).

## Choosing the structure

**Private limited company.** The default for ventures that intend to raise capital. Limited liability, free transferability of shares (within the articles), ESOPs, and the structure investors require. The trade-off is compliance: board meetings, filings, audits.

**Limited liability partnership (LLP).** Limited liability with a lighter compliance load and contractual flexibility through the LLP agreement. Excellent for professional services and family businesses; unsuitable for equity fundraising — VCs do not invest in LLPs.

**Sole proprietorship / partnership.** Fast and cheap, but no liability shield — the owners' personal assets stand behind business debts. Sensible only for very small, low-risk operations.

**One Person Company (OPC).** A single-founder company with limited liability; converts to a private limited as it grows.

The deciding question is rarely tax — it is **what the business will need in three years**: outside investors point to a company; a stable professional practice points to an LLP.

## Incorporation: the SPICe+ process

Company incorporation runs through the MCA's integrated **SPICe+** form:

1. **Name reservation** — check availability and trademark conflicts first; a name clash with a registered mark invites objections later.
2. **Digital signatures and DINs** for directors.
3. **Charter documents** — the Memorandum (objects) and Articles (internal rules). Standard templates work until they don't; investor-ready companies tailor the Articles.
4. **The SPICe+ filing** bundles PAN, TAN, EPFO, ESIC, profession tax and optional GST registration.
5. **Certificate of incorporation**, then a bank account, and **commencement-of-business declaration** (INC-20A) within 180 days.

With documents ready, the process completes in one to two weeks.

## The founders' agreement: the document that prevents lawsuits

Incorporation creates the company; the **founders' agreement** governs the founders. It should settle:

- **Equity split and vesting** — typically four-year vesting with a one-year cliff, so a departing founder doesn't leave with a dead block of equity
- **Roles and decision rights** — who decides what, and how deadlocks resolve
- **IP assignment** — everything built before and after incorporation belongs to the company
- **Exit and transfer restrictions** — right of first refusal, tag-along, what happens on death or incapacity
- **Non-compete and confidentiality** within enforceable bounds

Nearly every founder dispute we litigate traces to a missing or vague founders' agreement. The companion documents — employment agreements, an ESOP scheme, [well-drafted commercial contracts](/practices/corporate) with arbitration clauses (see our [arbitration guide](/insights/arbitration-india-beginners-guide)) — complete the foundation.

## Registrations and licences

Beyond incorporation, a typical business needs:

- **GST registration** — mandatory above turnover thresholds or for inter-state supply
- **Shops & Establishments registration** — state-specific, required for any premises; in Maharashtra, under the state's Shops and Establishments Act
- **Professional tax** (Maharashtra and several states)
- **MSME/Udyam registration** — optional but valuable: payment-protection and scheme benefits
- **Sector licences** — FSSAI for food, IEC for import-export, RBI/SEBI registrations for financial services, and so on
- **Trademark filing** — file early; the register is first-to-file in practice, and rebranding after traction is expensive

## People: employment and policies

From the first hire, employment law applies. Get four things right early:

1. **Written employment agreements** — probation, notice, confidentiality, IP assignment
2. **POSH compliance** — an Internal Committee and policy are mandatory once you have ten employees
3. **Statutory contributions** — EPF and ESI past the headcount thresholds
4. **Data protection** — the DPDP Act, 2023 imposes consent and security obligations on businesses handling personal data; build the basics in from day one

## The compliance calendar

A private limited company's recurring obligations: four board meetings a year, the AGM, annual MCA filings (AOC-4 financials, MGT-7 annual return), auditor appointment, statutory registers, and event-based filings for allotments, charges and director changes. Add GST returns, TDS, advance tax and labour filings. None of it is difficult; all of it is unforgiving of neglect, with per-day late fees and director-level consequences for persistent default.

## When disputes come

Commercial life produces disputes — recovery of dues, contract breaches, partner exits. Structure determines forum: shareholder oppression claims go to the [NCLT](/practices/tribunal), contract disputes to arbitration or the [civil courts](/insights/civil-litigation-india-guide), recovery below thresholds to summary procedures. The best protection is contracts drafted with the dispute in mind.
