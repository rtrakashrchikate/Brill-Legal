India has moved enormous areas of dispute resolution out of the civil courts and into specialised tribunals — insolvency to the NCLT, bank recovery to the DRT, consumer claims to the commissions, real-estate complaints to RERA. For litigants, this is mostly good news: faster procedure, specialist adjudicators, modest fees. But it makes the first question of any dispute — *where do I file?* — genuinely strategic. This guide maps the landscape. It anchors our [tribunal and regulatory practice](/practices/tribunal).

## Why tribunals exist

Tribunals trade the generality of civil courts for speed and specialisation. They are creatures of statute: each decides only what its parent Act assigns, follows simplified procedure (most are guided by natural justice rather than the full Civil Procedure Code), and sits with technical or expert members alongside judicial ones. Their orders bind like decrees and are enforced through their own machinery or the courts.

The trade-off: jurisdictional boundaries are rigid. A claim that straddles two statutes — a homebuyer with both RERA and consumer remedies, a creditor weighing DRT against insolvency — must pick its forum deliberately.

## The major tribunals at a glance

**NCLT — National Company Law Tribunal.** Company law and insolvency. Oppression and mismanagement petitions, mergers, and the entire **IBC** process: a financial or operational creditor's insolvency petition, the moratorium, resolution plans, liquidation. Appeals go to the **NCLAT**, then the Supreme Court. For founders and creditors alike, NCLT practice now rivals the commercial courts in importance — see our [business guide](/insights/starting-business-india-guide).

**DRT — Debt Recovery Tribunal.** Banks' and financial institutions' recovery claims above ₹20 lakh, and — critically for borrowers — challenges to **SARFAESI** enforcement: possession notices, auctions of secured property. A borrower aggrieved by a bank's measures files a Section 17 application here. Appeals to the **DRAT**, often with a pre-deposit.

**Consumer Commissions.** Three tiers — District (claims to ₹50 lakh), State (to ₹2 crore), National (above) — for deficiency in goods and services. Simple procedure, no court fee scaled to claim, and powers to order refund, replacement and compensation. Many homebuyer and insurance disputes live here.

**MahaRERA / REAT.** Real-estate project complaints — delay, refund, defects — covered in depth in our [RERA guide](/insights/rera-maharashtra-guide).

**NGT — National Green Tribunal.** Environmental disputes: project clearances, pollution, compensation. Strict 30/60-day limitation periods make speed essential.

**CAT — Central Administrative Tribunal.** Service disputes of central government employees — promotions, seniority, disciplinary action.

**MACT — Motor Accident Claims Tribunal.** Compensation for road-accident death and injury, computed on structured multiplier principles, recoverable from insurers.

**Tax tribunals (ITAT, GSTAT).** Appeals in income-tax and GST matters after the departmental stages; the GSTAT has finally begun functioning, completing the GST appeal chain.

**SAT — Securities Appellate Tribunal.** Appeals against SEBI orders — relevant to listed companies and market participants, and to our [white-collar practice](/practices/white-collar).

## Choosing the forum when statutes overlap

The recurring overlaps, and how we think about them:

- **Homebuyer: RERA vs consumer commission.** RERA is faster for delay/refund claims on registered projects; consumer fora suit claims emphasising compensation or unregistered projects. The Supreme Court confirms the remedies are concurrent — the choice is tactical.
- **Lender/creditor: DRT vs NCLT.** DRT recovers a debt; IBC threatens the debtor's control of the company itself. IBC is leverage, but it is collective — once admitted, the petitioning creditor shares the process with all creditors.
- **Shareholder: NCLT vs civil court vs arbitration.** Oppression claims are NCLT-exclusive; pure contract claims under a shareholders' agreement may belong in [arbitration](/insights/arbitration-india-beginners-guide). Drafting determines the battlefield.
- **Defective property: RERA vs civil suit.** Title disputes stay with the [civil courts](/insights/civil-litigation-india-guide); construction and delay grievances go to RERA.

Filing in the wrong forum is not fatal, but it costs months — and limitation keeps running.

## Appeals and the route back to the courts

Tribunals do not float free of the judiciary. Every tribunal has an appellate route — NCLAT, DRAT, NCDRC, REAT, and onward to the Supreme Court on law. And the High Courts retain **writ jurisdiction** under Articles 226/227 over tribunals acting without jurisdiction or in breach of natural justice. Pre-deposit requirements (commonly 25–50% in DRAT and consumer appeals) are a real strategic constraint when advising on whether to appeal.
