Consumer forums were created precisely because ordinary civil courts are too slow, too expensive, and too technical for everyday disputes about products and services. The Consumer Protection Act 2019 provides a three-tier system that any consumer can access — increasingly online — without necessarily engaging a lawyer.

## Choosing the right forum

The forum is determined by the value of the goods or services paid (not the compensation claimed):

### District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission
Claims where the value of goods or services does not exceed one crore rupees. This covers most individual consumer disputes — defective appliances, builder complaints, insurance claim rejections, telecom disputes.

### State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission
Claims between one crore and ten crore rupees, and appeals from the District Commission.

### National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission
Claims exceeding ten crore rupees, and appeals from the State Commission. The NCDRC sits in Delhi. The Supreme Court hears revisions from the NCDRC.

One common error is trying to inflate a complaint to a higher forum by including large consequential damages in the "value" of the claim. The value of the goods or services paid — not the total compensation sought — is the jurisdictional fact.

## What can be complained about

The Act covers:

- **Defective goods**: Goods that have a manufacturing defect, design defect, or depart from the standard contracted for
- **Deficiency in service**: Service that falls short of the standard promised or of what would reasonably be expected — including a service not delivered at all
- **Unfair trade practices**: False representations about goods or services, misleading advertisements, and similar conduct
- **Restrictive trade practices**: Practices that manipulate the market to the consumer's disadvantage

## The e-Daakhil process

The Ministry of Consumer Affairs operates the **e-Daakhil portal** for online filing of consumer complaints. The process:

1. **Register** on the e-Daakhil portal with email and mobile number
2. **Draft the complaint**: State the facts chronologically — the purchase, the defect or deficiency, the attempts to resolve with the seller or service provider, and the relief claimed
3. **Attach documents**: Purchase receipt/invoice, warranty card, correspondence with the seller, proof of payment, expert reports or photographs of defective goods
4. **Pay court fees**: Calculated on a slab basis on the value of the complaint; currently modest for lower-value claims
5. **Submit**: The complaint is filed and assigned a case number. The forum will issue notice to the opposite party

The opposite party has 30 days to file a reply. After replies and a brief hearing, the forum delivers its order.

## The mediation track

Under the 2019 Act, once a complaint is admitted, the forum may refer it to a Consumer Mediation Cell with the parties' consent. A trained mediator facilitates a settlement discussion. If successful, the settlement is confirmed as an order of the forum. If it fails, the complaint resumes the ordinary track. Mediation is faster for straightforward cases and is worth agreeing to where the primary goal is practical resolution.

## Common complaints by category

**Builders and real estate**: Delayed possession, carpet area shortfalls, defects in construction. RERA and the consumer forum have overlapping jurisdiction for real estate complaints; a consumer forum can award compensation for mental agony in addition to the specific RERA remedy.

**Insurance**: Repudiation of claims, partial settlement below contract terms. Insurance disputes are among the most successfully resolved in consumer forums.

**E-commerce and retail**: Non-delivery, counterfeit goods, refusal to honour warranty, misleading product descriptions.

**Banking and finance**: Unauthorised debit, failure to credit payment, charges not disclosed at the time of the contract.

**Telecom**: Billing disputes, service quality, wrongful disconnection.

## After the order

If the opposite party does not comply with the forum's order, the consumer can apply for enforcement. The forum has powers to direct attachment and sale of assets and, in persistent non-compliance, recommend prosecution for non-compliance with an order. Orders of the District and State Commissions can be appealed through the system; the time limit for appeal is 45 days from receipt of the order.
